# Project Evolution — voice-over script

For `project_evolution_explanation.mp4`. Written to be read aloud and used as
the subtitle track: one idea per sentence, no headings inside the narration.
Around 450 words, which lands near three minutes at a calm documentary pace.

---

In this new type of analysis, Project Evolution, your repository stops being a
list of commit messages and becomes something you can watch.

CodeXR replays the project through its own history, and every frame you see is
a complete analysis of the code exactly as it stood at that moment. Same
metrics, same mapping, same scale, from the first revision to the state you
are working on right now. So the city in front of you rises, spreads,
collapses and reorganises the way your project actually did.

You decide what the film is made of. Auto lets CodeXR choose for you: it
spreads the frames evenly in time rather than picking every Nth commit, so a
busy week does not eat the whole movie and a quiet month is still a month, and
it prefers merges and tags, because those are the moments where work really
landed. Range builds the film between two references you pick, when the story
you care about is one sprint, one feature, or one release. And Manual gives
you exact control: you name the commits, and those are the frames, in the
order you chose them. Whatever you pick, generating the movie takes one
button, and it all happens against your local git history. No remote, no
service, nothing leaves your machine.

Once the movie is built, it is yours to drive. Press play and it runs on its
own, each frame labelled with its commit and its date so you always know where
in the story you are standing. Pause whenever something catches your eye. Step
forward and back one frame at a time to compare two neighbouring commits.
Change the speed to half, normal or double, depending on whether you are
scanning years of work or studying a single week. And you can jump anywhere in
the timeline without waiting, because every frame is already analysed and
sitting there, ready.

While the movie is paused, the Field Mapping panel is right there, and this is
where it gets interesting. Change the chart, or change what the dimensions
mean: watch complexity grow instead of size, colour the city by language
instead of by risk. Nothing is interrupted, nothing is regenerated. Press play
again and the same history tells you a completely different story.

When you are done, clear the movie and generate another one, with different
frames, a different range, a different question in mind. Nothing stops you
from doing it as many times as you want.

Because that is the point of this analysis: a release history tells you what
happened. This shows you where it happened, and how your project got to be the
shape it is today.
